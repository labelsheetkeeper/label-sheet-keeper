Label Sheet Keeper for Word

A Word side panel that remembers which labels on a sheet are used, so you can finish partly used sheets.

Set it up on your Mac (once)

  1. Download manifest.xml
  2. Quit Word.
  3. In Finder, press ⌘⇧G and go to ~/Library/Containers/com.microsoft.Word/Data/Documents/
  4. If there's no folder named wef, make one (File › New Folder).
  5. Put manifest.xml inside wef.
  6. Open Word and any document. Click Label Sheet on the Home tab. If it isn't there, click Add-ins on the Home tab and choose Label Sheet Keeper.

Use it

  1. In the panel, choose your labels and click Make the sheet. Save the new document.
  2. On the map, click each label that's already peeled off your sheet.
  3. Fill labels: copy one you typed, place an address with a design, open the table window to fill many at once, or number them with a QR code or barcode.
  4. Print with ⌘P at 100% scale. Feed the sheet top edge first, print side up, and choose Labels as the paper type. The panel notices and asks whether the labels printed, then marks them used. Preview shows the page first, with the label edges drawn in.

On shipping label sheets, the panel can also put an Amazon return label PDF or any label picture on the next open label. It finds the label's border by itself.

From your phone: link your phone in the panel's Settings by scanning a QR code, then send a return label from your phone. It shows up in the panel, ready to print.

About partly used sheets: Avery says laser label sheets should go through a laser printer only once, because the heat softens the glue. The panel counts each sheet's trips through the printer, warns before a second trip, and helps you use up a sheet on its last trip. When there's more to print than a sheet holds, it offers to fill your other part-used sheets of that size before making new ones.

Several return labels: choose or drop several label PDFs at once, or use every page of one PDF. They wait in line and go on the sheet two at a time.

Lining up: print the test page, scan it on your printer's scanner (any way up), and the panel works out the printer adjustment from the scan. Works for every label size, sideways shipping labels included.

Addresses: one dropdown holds your last ten addresses and your address book. Each contact can have two addresses, two phones and two emails; tick which go on the label, for the whole sheet or just for that contact. Import from Contacts, Google, Outlook or Excel and choose who comes in. Put saved addresses in groups, like Family or Work, and fill a sheet with a whole group.

Labels saved: the panel counts the labels you print on partly used sheets instead of fresh ones. Hide the count in Settings any time; it keeps counting.

Label stock: the panel can count your sheets of each label, remind you when you're low, and reorder in one click. Amazon and Walmart links add the labels to your cart; other stores open the product page.

Backups: Settings › Backup saves your address book, return address, sizes, adjustments and stock in a Word document you can restore on any Mac.

Address suggestions

Type a house number and street in any of the panel's address boxes, in the table window, or on a label in the Word document, and matching US and Canadian addresses are suggested. Pick one to fill in the street, city, state or province, and ZIP or postal code. For a label in the document, the suggestions show at the bottom of the panel. A ZIP code or postal code on its own also fills in the city.

Suggestions come from Geoapify through the Label Sheet Keeper server, so you don't need an account. When the day's shared lookups are used up, they come from Photon by Komoot, a free service with no sign-up. Both use OpenStreetMap data, and what you type is sent to them. Some house numbers aren't in OpenStreetMap yet. You can turn suggestions off in Settings › Address suggestions.

Bring your own key (optional). For your own daily allowance, use a free Geoapify key instead:

  1. Go to geoapify.com and sign up for a free account. No credit card is needed; the free plan includes 3,000 lookups a day.
  2. Create a project and copy its API key.
  3. In the key's settings, limit it to requests from this add-in by adding https://labelsheetkeeper.github.io as an allowed origin (or HTTP referrer). Then the key won't work on other websites.
  4. In Word, open the panel, then Settings › Address suggestions. Paste the key and click Save key. The panel checks it with Geoapify.

The key is kept by the panel on your Mac only. It isn't in this project's code or in your Word documents. With a key, suggestions go straight to Geoapify on your own allowance instead of the shared one. If you use Word on another Mac, paste the key there too.

Canadian addresses are written the way Canada Post asks: 5-123 Main St for a unit and Toronto ON  M5V 3L9 on the last line. Mail to Canada gets a CANADA line. If you mail from Canada, choose Settings › You mail from › Canada, and US addresses get a USA line instead.

Label sizes

  • 1″ × 2⅝″, 30 per sheet (Avery 5160 laser, 8160 inkjet). Blank sheet
  • ½″ × 1¾″, 80 per sheet (Avery 5167 laser, 8167 inkjet). Blank sheet
  • 2″ × 4″, 10 per sheet (Avery 5163 laser, 8163 inkjet). Blank sheet
  • 3⅓″ × 4″, 6 per sheet (Avery 5164 laser, 8164 inkjet). Blank sheet
  • Shipping labels, 2 per sheet, shown sideways (Avery 5126 laser, 8126 inkjet). Blank sheet
  • 8½″ × 11″, 1 per sheet (Avery 5165 laser, 8165 inkjet). Blank sheet

Use the laser version of a label on a laser printer. To add a size, use Add your own size in the panel, or edit formats.js in the GitHub repository.
